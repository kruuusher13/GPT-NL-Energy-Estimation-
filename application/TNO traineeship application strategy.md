Romir Malik should apply to the TNO Traineeship as someone who turns technical work into decisions, not as a data scientist who wants a broader job. Before **Sunday 4 October, 23:59**, he needs five fixes: bring the assignment page under 200 words (it counts 207 including its header); correct two overclaims ("GPT-NL's compute costs" and "AI above all"); replace the letter's thinnest paragraph with evidence of ownership; add his finding that summing per-task energy records overstates parallel runs **2–11×**, as proof he questions the status quo; and reorder the CV so business-facing work and extracurricular leadership show on the first screen. ## Eight places, three rotations and a decisive day in Leiden

The TNO Traineeship is one generalist programme with no thematic tracks. **Over two years, trainees hold three positions chosen from more than 60 TNO departments.** For three and a half days a week they work "in a role as a project leader, business developer or consultant". About one day a week goes to development: a personal coach, peer consulting, courses and **TNO-wide strategic projects for half a day a week** ([TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/); [TNO landing page](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/)). Pay is **€3,530 gross a month**, plus 8% holiday pay, a 13th month of 8.33% and a flexible benefits budget. The job is based in The Hague with hybrid working, at 40 hours a week ([TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/); [Magnet.me](https://magnet.me/nl-NL/vacature/1082920/tno-traineeship---1st-of-february--2027)). Trainees can create positions together with managers instead of filling existing vacancies. The programme is partly "by trainees for trainees", with committees that organise workshops and events ([Tobias Mooy story](https://www.tno.nl/en/newsroom/insights/2025/04/time-setter-story-tobias-mooy-tno/)). Romir's event-building experience fits there. The current FAQ offers **8 positions per starting date**, up from "6 to 8" in the previous cycle ([FAQ-en](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq-en/); [old FAQ](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq)). Each intake joins "an enthusiastic group of nearly 30 trainees" ([TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/)).

The application is three PDFs of at most 5 MB each: a CV, a motivation letter, and a one-page assignment of no more than 200 words. **The assignment questions changed this round.** "How will you make an impact?" and "What do you want to learn about yourself?" have been replaced by "Which talents would you like to develop during the Traineeship?" and "How does this contribute to your development and your long-term ambition?" ([FAQ-en](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq-en/); [Sept 2026 vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/04/tno-traineeship-1-september-2026)). The landing page still mentions "impact and learning goals", so the live vacancy is the version to trust ([TNO landing page](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/)). After the deadline, an outside partner runs "questionnaires about your personality, motivations and values". The current FAQ calls this partner "lxly"; the previous cycle used LTP. The Selection Day is "a mandatory and important part" of the procedure, with **two interviews, a group assignment and a short pitch** ([FAQ-en](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq-en/); [old FAQ](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq)).

### Group assignment: make the group better, visibly

The 2019 group task was a debate about which of two people should go to Mars ([Glassdoor 2019](https://www.glassdoor.com.mx/Entrevista/TNO-Entrevista-E257744-RVW30545311.htm)). Whatever the 2026 version is, it will probably be an open problem with no right answer and a time limit. The behaviours to show are the ones TNO names itself: connecting disciplines, valuing different perspectives, communicating well and taking responsibility ([TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/)). In practice, he should propose a simple process early: agree the criteria, weigh the options, decide. If someone else proposes a good one, he should back it. He should take a role that serves the group, such as timekeeper or summariser, rather than grabbing the chair. He should bring quieter members in by name. When he disagrees, he should say what would change his mind, and when someone does change it, he should say so out loud. And he should make sure the group reaches a decision on time.

The trap for a strong analyst is to become the group's critic, piling on technical caveats. One well-chosen fact does more than five objections. Current trainees help run the day ([Charlot spotlight](https://tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/trainee-spotlight-charlot); [Inez story](https://www.tno.nl/en/newsroom/insights/2026/08/time-setter-story-inez-erwin-magino/)), so he should treat lunch and the meet-and-greet as part of the day too.

## Raise screening, permits and Dutch before TNO has to ask

### The VGB-B lookback is the one risk that could override everything

TNO's current FAQ says that, besides a certificate of good conduct, "a VGB-B screening by the Algemene Inlichtingen- en Veiligheidsdienst is necessary" for all trainees. The vacancy says only that it "may be necessary", tied to defence and security clients ([FAQ-en](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq-en/); [TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/)). Screening is not limited to defence units. A Corporate Strategy Office analyst role requires an AIVD certificate of no objection because the office works for defence and security clients ([Strategy Analyst vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/07/strategy-analyst/)). The AIVD's published policy sets three rules that matter here. B-level screening **looks back eight years**. Any continuous stay abroad of six months or more creates a "witte vlek" (blank spot), and without a cooperating foreign service the certificate "is in principle refused". Exceptions require, among other conditions, **residence in the Netherlands for at least half the assessment period** ([AIVD Beleidsregel 2018](https://www.aivd.nl/binaries/aivd_nl/documenten/publicaties/2018/02/19/beleidsregel-veiligheidsonderzoeken/Beleidsregel+VO.pdf)).

Romir has lived in the Netherlands since roughly the start of his MSc in September 2025; he should use his exact arrival date. That gives him about 13 months by the Selection Day and 17 by the start date, against the four years the exception requires. The outcome therefore depends on whether the AIVD can check his years in India through cooperating channels, and nothing public answers that. The policy dates from 2018 and may have been revised. TNO has published nothing on how it handles recent non-EU arrivals.

So he should ask, and soon. The obvious objection is that raising it could cost him a Selection Day invitation. But the FAQ already states the requirement, and his residence history will appear on the screening forms anyway. The worst outcome would be an offer withdrawn in December, after he has resigned from his jobs. Raising it directly also shows the ownership TNO says it wants. He should call **Serge Moesman**, Corporate Recruiter, on +31 6 10 93 35 86 on Monday 5 or Tuesday 6 October, after submitting, and confirm the conversation by email ([TNO vacancy](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/vacancies/2026/09/tno-traineeship-1st-february-2027/)). A script:

> "I applied on 4 October for the February 2027 Traineeship and wanted to raise a practical point early rather than late. The FAQ says all trainees need a VGB-B. I'm an Indian national and have lived in the Netherlands since [month] 2025, so most of the eight-year lookback is abroad. How does TNO handle that? Is the screening needed before the start for every trainee, or only for certain rotations, and how have candidates with a short Dutch history fared? I'd also like to understand the permit route for a recent Utrecht graduate."

The recruiter might say the screening happens later or at a different level. He might say early rotations are limited to departments without defence clients, or that the screening outcome decides the offer. Each answer tells Romir how much to invest. When the forms come, he should fill them in completely and consistently with his CV, including the ISRO and CSIR internships. If any screening was done for his thesis placement, he should say so.

### The permit route works because he is a recent Dutch graduate

TNO's FAQ says it "may be able to support" non-EU residents in getting a residence and work permit. TNO is listed in the IND's public register of recognised sponsors for research, under KvK number 27376655. The research could not check its listing in the separate register for highly skilled migrants, because that page was cut off ([FAQ-en](https://www.tno.nl/en/careers/graduates/tno-traineeship/faq-en/); [IND research register](https://ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors/public-register-research); [IND work register](https://ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors/public-register-work)). Salary decides the route. Non-EU graduates of Dutch institutions can apply for an **orientation year (zoekjaar)**, which lets them work without a separate work permit. Those who then move to highly-skilled-migrant status get a **reduced salary threshold of €3,122 gross a month (2026)**, compared with €4,357 for under-30s and €5,942 for those 30 and over ([Leiden International Centre](https://www.leideninternationalcentre.nl/get-advice/blogs/the-dutch-orientation-year-zoekjaar-a-practical-guide-for-international-graduates)). **The trainee salary of €3,530 clears the reduced threshold but not the standard one**, so his route depends on that recent-graduate status. Before the 16 November terms talk, he should confirm three things with ind.nl and with TNO: which permit he will hold on 1 February 2027 and when it ends, whether TNO will sponsor a highly-skilled-migrant application, and the 2027 thresholds once they are published. This research could not confirm the eligibility window for the orientation year or the IND's processing times.

## Conclusion

Romir's advantage is not on paper, because everyone in the Leiden room will have cleared the same paper bar. His real edge is that he has already done a TNO project and drawn, unprompted, the conclusion the Traineeship is built around: a technical answer is worth something only when someone acts on it. TNO's vision statement says the same, so do its trainees' accounts of their rotations, and so does the list of roles in the vacancy. Every edit in this plan serves one goal: to make that conclusion the thread through the CV, the letter, the one-pager, the pitch and the interviews, with his technical depth kept visible as the evidence behind it.

Two things about the odds matter more than polish. First, the screening question could override everything, but he can find out within a week by asking. Asking costs little, while discovering a problem after resigning from two jobs would cost a lot. Second, this round is not his only chance. The two-year graduation rule keeps him eligible for the September 2027 intake (Selection Day 28 May 2027), although his orientation-year clock and the reduced salary threshold make timing matter, and better Dutch plus more experience working with stakeholders would strengthen a second attempt. With 8 places, even a flawless application can fail. He should prepare as if this is the round and plan as if it might not be.
