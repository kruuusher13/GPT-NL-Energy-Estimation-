Dear Mr Moesman,

I am a data scientist, and for three years I have worked on one question at every size of computer: what will this computation cost, and can we know before we spend it?

At ISRO the answer lived on a single chip. I moved object detection onto the FPGA of a satellite, under 15 ms a frame, and found that the bottleneck was not the kernel I had been asked to speed up but the data path around it. At CSIR it was one vehicle: perception for an underwater robot that inspects dams so divers do not have to, where I chose the design with one failure mode fewer over the one with the better benchmark. At TNO it was the national supercomputer. Inside the GPT-NL programme I built a tool that predicts the energy, cost and CO2 of a data-curation run on Snellius before it is submitted, to 2.8% on its dominant stage, and showed that the usual way of adding up per-task energy overstates a parallel run two to eleven times.

Each time, the answer sat outside the part I was handed. That is why I am applying for the Traineeship rather than a specialist role. My next scale is not a bigger machine; it is the people, partners and decisions around it, which is exactly where TNO turns knowledge into practice.

I have spent my first year in the Netherlands on the other side of the table too. As founding engineer of Torxflow I took the product through UtrechtInc's validation programme by testing it with prospective customers before building more. At ConcertLab I work directly with the CEO on the four-year financial model behind a live activation round. As Head of External Relations of the Utrecht Data Science Community I built partnerships with more than ten organisations and grew attendance by 40%, alongside Utrecht's Honours Programme in Leadership. And in a course tournament my 38.9M-parameter chess model took first place against 7B-parameter baselines, by changing the formulation rather than the size.

What I want from the Traineeship is the practice none of this can give me: leading a project where I am not the expert, building a case with partners who owe me nothing, and advising on decisions far larger than one model, with a coach and peers who tell me where I fall short. TNO's strategy for 2026–2029 puts strategic autonomy at its centre. Europe's AI will be paid for in energy, euros and grid capacity, and I want to help make that cost visible before it is spent.

Julio de Oliveira Filho, who supervised my thesis at TNO, has agreed to act as a reference. My assignment links a 30-second film of this path. I live in Utrecht, am learning Dutch, and can start on 1 February 2027.
